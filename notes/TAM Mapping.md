# FlytBase TAM Map: Who Buys Autonomous Drone Software, and Where to Start

**Bottom line:** Five segments, about 20% of the 27 mapped, hold roughly 80% of FlytBase's near-term opportunity: electric grid utilities (distribution and transmission), solar farm owners, oil & gas (refineries and upstream/offshore), and private security firms. Your plan to start with grid operators and then do oil & gas is right. One correction matters, though. In the US, the biggest docked-drone utility deal so far (Dominion Energy, 100 docks) went to Skydio, a hardware rival. US rules on Chinese-made DJI drones also make new DJI-based deals harder. So aim Campaign 1 first at grid operators in Europe, Asia-Pacific, Latin America and the Middle East. Pick US utilities one by one.

## TL;DR

- **Where the money is:** Six segments make up about 81% of my illustrative 24-month revenue estimate (≈$4.2M of ≈$5.2M across the top 10). They are electric distribution/integrated utilities, solar, electric transmission, refineries/petrochemicals, upstream/offshore oil & gas, and private security. FlytBase already has proof points in each one: Statnett, Xcel, EnBW, Shell, Titan Protection and MSS Security.
- **How big the outbound job is:** This is a small, "surgical" target list. The top 10 segments add up to about 4,200 qualified accounts and roughly 25,000 contacts, which is under the 30,000 line. After enrichment, expect only 60–80% to be reachable. So use account-based, one-by-one outreach, not mass email.
- **What could change the picture:** The US BVLOS rule (Part 108, RIN 2120-AL82) is still listed on reginfo.gov as "Pending Review" at the White House; it arrived on July 10, 2026, passed the 90-day review mark on Oct 8, 2026, and has not been published. The FCC's December 2025 "Covered List" blocks new foreign drone models in the US. Both affect US timing, so lead with non-US grid and oil & gas accounts, plus US accounts that already run docks.

## Plain-English Glossary

| Term                               | What it means (simple)                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TAM / SAM / SOM**                | TAM = everyone who could ever buy. SAM = the part you can actually serve, given your product, regions and rules. SOM = the part you can realistically win soon.                                               |
| **NAICS code**                     | A 6-digit government code for "what industry a business is in" (North American Industry Classification System, 2022 version). Example: 221121 = electric transmission. Data tools use it to filter companies. |
| **Drone-in-a-box (DiaB) / dock**   | A weatherproof box that a drone lives in. The drone takes off on its own, flies a mission, lands, and recharges. No pilot needs to be on site.                                                                |
| **BVLOS**                          | "Beyond Visual Line of Sight." The drone flies farther than a person on the ground can see. Docks only pay off when BVLOS is allowed.                                                                         |
| **VLOS**                           | Visual Line of Sight. The pilot or an observer must see the drone.                                                                                                                                            |
| **Part 107 / Part 108**            | US FAA drone rules. Part 107 is today's rule; BVLOS needs a waiver. Part 108 is the proposed rule that would make BVLOS routine. It is not law yet.                                                           |
| **OIRA**                           | The White House office that reviews big rules before they are published.                                                                                                                                      |
| **FCC Covered List**               | A US list of equipment that can't get _new_ radio approval. All foreign-made drones were added in Dec 2025. Drones already approved can still be used.                                                        |
| **ROC (Remote Operations Center)** | One control room that runs many docks in many places.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **TSO / DSO**                      | TSO = Transmission System Operator: runs the high-voltage "highways" of the grid (e.g., Statnett). DSO = Distribution System Operator: runs the local "streets" of the grid down to homes.                    |
| **IOU / Co-op / Public power**     | US utility types. IOU = investor-owned (big, stock-listed). Co-op = owned by its customers. Public power = city- or state-owned.                                                                              |
| **Substation**                     | A fenced yard of transformers and switches that changes voltage. There are about 55,000 in the US. Most have no staff on site.                                                                                |
| **CAGR**                           | Compound Annual Growth Rate: the average yearly growth rate. A 20% CAGR means the market grows about 20% each year.                                                                                           |
| **ACV / ARR**                      | ACV = Annual Contract Value (what one customer pays per year). ARR = Annual Recurring Revenue (total yearly subscription revenue).                                                                            |
| **NERC CIP**                       | Mandatory North American grid security rules. CIP-014 covers physical security of key substations; other CIP standards cover cybersecurity.                                                                   |
| **PHMSA**                          | US agency that regulates pipeline safety.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **LDAR / OGI**                     | Leak Detection and Repair / Optical Gas Imaging: methane-leak checks, often with special cameras.                                                                                                             |
| **DFR**                            | Drone as First Responder: a police or fire drone that flies to a 911 call before officers arrive.                                                                                                             |
| **VMS / SCADA**                    | VMS = video management software for CCTV (e.g., Genetec, Milestone). SCADA = the control system that runs industrial equipment.                                                                               |
| **ISPS / MTSA**                    | Port and ship security rules (global / US).                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **ATEX**                           | EU rules for explosive-atmosphere zones, like refineries.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Enrichment**                     | Using tools such as Apollo to add emails and phone numbers to a list of names.                                                                                                                                |
| **ICP**                            | Ideal Customer Profile: a description of your best-fit customer.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Pareto (80/20)**                 | The idea that a few things (about 20%) drive most of the results (about 80%).                                                                                                                                 |

## How to Use NAICS Codes for List Building (Beginner Guide)

- **What it is:** The US Census Bureau gives every business type a code. The first 2 digits are the broad sector (22 = Utilities); 6 digits is the exact industry (221121 = Electric Bulk Power Transmission and Control).
- **How to use it in Apollo or ZoomInfo:** Filter companies by NAICS or SIC code, _and_ by industry keywords ("utility," "transmission," "substation"). Then add filters for size (employees or revenue) and country.
- **Warning:** Data vendors often assign codes wrongly. A utility might be tagged "energy" or "renewables." Use the code as one filter, not the only one.
- **Better trick for utilities and oil & gas:** Start from an official list and import it as company names or domains. Good lists: the EIA-861 utility list, the ENTSO-E member list, the PHMSA operator list, the EIA refinery list, and the MSHA mine dataset. These lists are complete and free.

### Why each top segment scored the way it did

- **#1 Distribution/integrated utilities (38):** Huge asset base: about 55,000 US substations, 6.3 million miles of distribution lines, 153 million customers (DOE). Most substations have no staff. Big deals are proven: DroneXL reported on Oct 16, 2026, citing Skydio CEO Adam Bry, that Dominion Energy added 100 docked drones; in Dominion's own film the utility says checking a substation by docked drone before crews roll out "saves 8 to 12 hours per event," and that its program will grow "from two dedicated pilots to six to eight within two to three years." Slow cycle (score 4) because of security reviews and NERC CIP.
- **#2 Solar (38):** Lots of remote sites, simple repeat missions (thermal scans), and strong FlytBase proof. FlytBase's EnBW case study says EnBW scaled inspections from 150 MW to 1 GW and moved "from annual to quarterly monitoring" (up to 4× more inspections on the same budget) using DJI Dock 2, FlytBase and Sitemark, though this was a "proof-of-concept deployment with two operational docks." Xcel Energy runs DJI docks with FlytBase on solar farms. Faster cycle, but smaller deals.
- **#3 Transmission/TSOs (36):** Few buyers (only 40 TSOs in Europe), but each is very large. Statnett (11,000 km of lines, 150 substations) runs FlytBase from its Drone Operations Center in Sunndalsøra. It acted after a suspected sabotage oil leak at the Bærum transformer station in March 2025. Low education needed: TSOs already understand drones.
- **#4 Refineries (36):** Dense, high-value sites with dangerous areas. Shell Rotterdam flies docks 12 hours a day (expanding to 24/7) for tank roofs, valves, gas detection and emergency response. Permits took 6–7 months, which slows the cycle.
- **#5 Upstream/offshore (36):** Shell is flying drone-in-a-box from its Mars floating platform in the Gulf. The Journal of Petroleum Technology (SPE) reported on Sept 11, 2026 that Shell "became the first operator in the region to secure FAA approval for self-approved offshore beyond-visual-line-of-sight flights," after trials at Mars in April 2026. Bloomberg reported on Oct 9, 2026 that Shell estimates the project could save nearly $50M over three years by restarting production a day sooner after storms, with the first drone due on Mars before the end of 2026 and at least five more in 2027. Big money, but few buyers, and each moves carefully.
- **#6 Private security (35):** Fastest cycle (score 8). Security firms sell "drone patrol" to their clients and buy quickly. Several FlytBase customers already: Titan Protection, MSS Security, 24-7 Drone Force, Premier Security. Low deal value (score 4), because many can use the $999/year Pro plan.
- **#7 Mining (33):** Giant sites with safety and security needs. FlytBase has Anglo American (Peru), SQM (Chile; a 678 km² mine), and Kansanshi (Zambia). Fewer buyers.
- **#8 Police (31):** Many agencies (17,541 in the US) and a hot use case (DFR). But in the US, Flock Safety, Skydio and BRINC dominate, and DJI is restricted, so fit is weaker there. Stronger in Europe and Latin America (Westkust Police, Uruguay Ministry of Interior).
- **#9–10 Ports and Pipelines (30):** Good fit (Singapore MPA for ports). Slower, more regulated. Pipelines have strong methane-rule drivers, but Percepto already holds an EPA approval.

## Segment Deep Dives (Top 10)

### 1. Electric Distribution & Integrated Utilities (Campaign 1)

|Topic|Details|
|---|---|
|**Pain points**|Thousands of unstaffed substations. Crew trucks driven out just to "go look." Storm damage checks are slow. Physical attacks: 13 substation attacks were investigated over a few weeks in late 2022 (Utility Dive), including the Dec 3, 2022 shooting at two Duke Energy substations in Moore County, North Carolina, which the FBI says caused an outage for approximately 45,000 customers. Wildfire risk.|
|**What they use now**|Truck rolls, foot patrols, helicopters for lines, CCTV and fences, manual drone pilots, drone service providers. Competing docks: Skydio (Dominion, ComEd, Southern Company early access), Percepto (Florida Power & Light: 13 drones initially, with plans for "hundreds").|
|**Buying triggers**|A substation attack or sabotage. A big storm or outage. A wildfire plan. A rate case with grid-hardening money. A new UAS program manager hire. Part 108 being published. Dominion-style peer news.|
|**Success metrics (KPIs)**|Truck rolls avoided. Hours saved per event (Dominion: 8–12 hours). Time from alarm to eyes-on. Inspections per substation per month. Thermal faults found early. Cost per inspection. Outage minutes (SAIDI).|
|**Compliance**|NERC CIP-014 (physical security of critical substations; FERC ordered these standards after the 2013 Metcalf attack). NERC CIP cyber standards: data and networks near substations must be secure, which is why on-prem/air-gapped options and SOC 2/ISO 27001 matter. FAA Part 107 plus BVLOS waivers. EU: national aviation authority "specific category" approvals. Data residency.|
|**Tech maturity**|High at large IOUs and TSOs (they have drone teams and GIS/SCADA). Low at small municipal utilities and co-ops.|

|Topic|Details|
|---|---|
|**Pain points**|Huge sites. Panel faults and lost output. Copper and panel theft. Sending crews out for quarterly inspections is expensive.|
|**What they use now**|Quarterly drone service flights, manual thermal scans, CCTV, guards.|

### 3. Electric Transmission (TSOs / Transmission Owners)

## Outbound Playbook Notes

**1. Is this a small or large TAM?** Small. It's surgical.

|Step|Math (estimates)|
|---|---|
|Qualified accounts, top 10|≈4,200|
|Relevant contacts per account|≈6 (buyer, user, security, IT/OT, innovation, procurement)|
|Total contacts|≈4,200 × 6 ≈ **25,000** → under 30,000 = **surgical / enterprise approach**|

|Reachable after enrichment (60–80%)|Grid: ≈3,600–7,200 contacts. Top 10: ≈15,000–20,000|

**2. What "surgical" means in practice:**

- Use account-based outreach. Research each account. Write a personal first line, for example: "Saw your new substation security program after the X incident."
- Run multi-threading: contact 3–6 people per account (operations user + security + innovation + IT/OT).
- Use small batches, about 50–100 accounts per week, not thousands.
- Mix channels: email, LinkedIn, partner intros, events (Energy Drone & Robotics Summit, NestGen, Commercial UAV Expo).

**3. Suggested Campaign 1 order (grid):**

- **Wave A (fastest):** TSOs and large DSOs in Europe (ENTSO-E members), APAC (TNB-like utilities), LatAm and the Middle East. DJI hardware is allowed there, and Statnett is a strong peer story.
- **Wave B:** US IOUs that already run docks or have FAA BVLOS waivers. Pitch FlytBase as the hardware-agnostic "one control room" layer that works across mixed fleets.
- **Wave C:** Large US public power and G&T co-ops (cheaper Pro-plan pilots).

**4. Campaign 2 (oil & gas):** Start with refineries and offshore operators near Shell's peer group (majors and national oil companies), using the Shell story. Then midstream pipelines.

**5. Message angles that match the data:**

- Grid: "See inside any substation in minutes, without a truck roll." Use Dominion-type numbers (8–12 hours saved per event) as industry proof, and Statnett as FlytBase proof.
- O&G: "Turn quarterly inspections into daily ones," plus Shell's storm-restart economics.
- Security: "Replace guard patrol hours; alarm-to-video in minutes."

## Recommendations

1. **Keep the grid as Campaign 1, but start outside the US.** Statnett-type TSOs and big DSOs in Europe and APAC have less hardware risk and a strong FlytBase story.
2. **Add solar to Campaign 1.** Integrated utilities (like Xcel) own solar too. It closes faster and has FlytBase's best numbers (EnBW).
3. **Run oil & gas as Campaign 2, led by the Shell stories** (Rotterdam onshore, Mars offshore). Refineries first.
4. **Open a partner-led security lane** in parallel. It brings fast revenue with little sales effort.
5. **Build lists from official registries** (EIA-861, ENTSO-E, PHMSA, EIA refineries, MSHA), then enrich in Apollo. Don't rely only on NAICS filters.
6. **Treat Skydio and Percepto as the main US grid and oil & gas rivals.** Position FlytBase as the neutral software layer for mixed fleets.
