### Product evaluation

To understand how the market perceives your product it's done by asking 3 crucial Questions.

1. is your product market **hyper-saturated with competing outreach?**
   
2. is it a **demand capturing product**? (where the need already exists, urgency can't be created) (ex- cybersecurity, recruitment)
   
   **demand generation product** ? (clients don't exactly know what they need but unanimously agree to the outcome(better revenue, better reach, saved time) it can be a unique saas
   
1. **how differentiated is your solution?** (if you are one of the 20 companies doing what you do, your product will sell itself- outbound isn't necessary)

### Market Evaluation

1. is your market saturated?- (ex: e-commerce, agency services)
		you'll need a more compelling offer, shorter email and sharp differentiator
		Because your email would be competing with a dozen of other in the same inbox daily (fighting for attention).

2. is your market unsaturated? (ex: niche manufacturing, specialized services)
		you get the liberty of slightly longer length and can lean harder on the strengths of the offer itself.
		Because the prospect isn't desensitized.


## Chapter 2-A: ICP Definition


ICP means the **detailed definition of a company** and the **exact persona within that company** who is willing to buy your product and become a best customer.

ideal customer profile is not an e-commerce brand or SaaS company- those are markets. 

 pick one segment, then write its ICP with these five traits, then name 3 decision-maker personas, then group them by shared pain (PQS).

#### Traits of a best customer (5 ESSENTIALS)

##### 1. know what team structure do they have?
   
	What's the ideal team structure that makes them most likely to be YOUR good client and more likely to show buying signals.
	
**How it proves best fit:** It provides proof of **operational capacity and need**. If an organization does not have the departments or team size required to implement, manage, or derive ROI from your solution.
	
they cannot succeed with your offer. Matching this structure proves they have the exact internal infrastructure capable of adopting and using your product.

##### 2. identify Buying Signals

	try to gauge what situation your client is facing when they come to buy your product.
	
-  Are they hiring?
- Losing employees? 
- Just raised funding? 
- What's happening within their company that creates the need?
    
- **How it proves best fit:** It provides proof of **active urgency and timing**. Many companies fit demographic criteria on paper but are in maintenance mode and will not buy.
  
  Identifying these signals proves the prospect is facing the specific friction that forces budget allocation and creates an immediate need to solve the problem today.

##### 3. Decision-Maker Profile
   
- Who do you actually speak to on calls?
- What's their title, department, reporting structure? 
- What tech do they typically need to have in place? Shopify, HubSpot, whatever it may be?

**How it proves best fit:** It proves **authority and technical compatibility**.

- Reaching the verified title and reporting level ensures the prospect has actual buying authority and direct ownership over the problem, avoiding conversations with gatekeepers who cannot make financial decisions.
    
- Matching the exact tech stack proves the solution will integrate with their current workflow rather than creating technical blockers that stall closed-won deals
