
# ICP

## 1. TEAM STRUCTURE
	
		. prospect company needs specific ground infrastructure, field headcount, and physical site distribution to justify and run automated drone flights.

**How it proves an enterprise is a best fit:**

- the prospect company should have a dedicated security patrol units, environmental compliance teams, field asset inspection crews, or GIS/spatial data specialists.
    
- If a mining or port facility has no in-house field operations, safety officers, or technical dispatch team, they will lack the internal capacity to deploy dock stations, process telemetry or maintain drone hardware.
    
- Matching this structure proves the enterprise has the physical scale and personnel required to adopt the platform.
  
## 2. Buying Signals & Company Situation

- **Why it is needed for FlytBase:** Enterprise deals have long sales cycles. You cannot pitch autonomous robotics if the company is not actively experiencing an operational shift, incident, or technological mandate.
    
- **How it proves an enterprise is a best fit:**
    
    - **Hiring Triggers:** 
	      1. Job posts mentioning keywords like "FAA Part 107," "BVLOS" (Beyond Visual Line of Sight), "UAS Program," "Robotics Operations," or "Remote Sensing". This proves budget is already allocated for aerial programs.
        
	- **Expansion / Perimeter Changes:** 
		  Physical site expansions, facility modernization grants, port automation initiatives, or critical infrastructure security mandates.
        
	- **Adverse Incidents:** 
		  Security breaches at perimeter fencing, regulatory fines for delayed pipeline/flare inspection, or worker safety hazards in hazardous zones.
        
    - These signals prove **timing and active budget urgency**, transforming a cold account into an urgent demand scenario.
        

### 3. Decision-Maker Profile

- **Why it is needed for FlytBase:** Because the buyer's title varies wildly (Head of Security, Innovation Manager, Drone Lead, Operations Director), job titles alone cannot define the persona.
    
- **How it proves the contact is the right champion:**
    
    - **Tech Stack & Hardware Proof:** Analyzing past successful deals reveals what hardware and software they must already use—e.g., DJI Dock, Skydio Dock, Esri ArcGIS, Milestone Systems (VMS), or automated perimeter sensor platforms. A contact actively managing these specific tools proves operational ownership.
        
    - **Functional Ownership:** Instead of searching by static title, you profile the decision-maker by their reporting line and day-to-day KPIs (e.g., perimeter surveillance response times, asset downtime, pilot headcount reduction). If a person oversees that metric, it proves they have direct economic interest and buying authority, regardless of whether their title says "Robotics" or "Site Safety".
        

### 4. Common Pain Points

- **Why it is needed for FlytBase:** A Head of Security and a Mining Operations Manager look at a drone dock through completely different lenses.
    
- **How it proves best fit:**
    
    - By grouping historical closed deals into **Pain-Qualified Segments (PQS)**, you identify the exact operational bottleneck that forced them to buy:
        
        - _Security PQS:_ Slow perimeter dispatch times, blind spots in 10,000-acre facilities, high cost of manual guard patrols.
            
        - _Asset Integrity PQS:_ Dangerous manual inspections on high-voltage flare stacks or conveyor belts, production downtime during asset checks.
            
    - Proving an enterprise shares these identical operational risks allows you to align cold outreach directly with their daily challenges, validating problem-solution fit before sending a message.
        

### 5. Past Client Case Studies (Lookalike Modeling)

- **Why it is needed for FlytBase:** Selling high-ticket robotics to risk-averse enterprise operators requires concrete, direct proof of success.
    
- **How it proves an enterprise is a best fit:**
    
    - By anchoring to your existing flagship clients (e.g., a specific port authority, an open-pit copper mine, or an oil refinery), you extract their firmographic and geospatial parameters: site acreage, facility classification, regulatory environment, and geographical footprint.
        
    - You then use lookalike tools (like [Ocean](https://ocean.io) or [Apify](https://apify.com)) to map every twin enterprise across the region.
        
    - Matching the exact lookalike profile of an already successful implementation proves technical viability, simplifies compliance hurdles (e.g., shared regulatory frameworks like BVLOS waivers), and provides immediate, industry-adjacent social proof.
